## Slide 3 — Selected City: Guangzhou
**Why Guangzhou?**

- Major economic hub in South China, dense population (~18 million)
- Subtropical monsoon climate → abundant rainfall (~1,700mm annually)
- Long rainy season (April–September), frequent typhoons
- Rapid urban expansion → natural ground replaced by concrete
- Guangzhou has already joined China's national Sponge City program

---

## Slide 4 — Problem Statement: Guangzhou's Water Problems
**A city in two minds: too much water vs. not enough**

| Problem | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| 🌊 **Urban Flooding** | Heavy rainfall overwhelms drainage; too much concrete, no ground for rainwater to soak into |
| 💧 **Water Shortage** | Rainwater flows away too quickly and is never stored or reused; dry seasons bring缺水 |
| 🏙️ **Urbanization makes it worse** | Buildings and roads replaced farmland and wetlands → natural water absorption capacity dropped |

> *Data point: In 2020, over 400 rivers in China flooded within two months, affecting 50 million people*

---

## Slide 5 — What Is a Sponge City?
**Core concept: Let the city act like a sponge**

Instead of fighting water, the city **welcomes** it:

```
   Absorb → Store → Release (when needed)
```

**Two types of infrastructure work together:**

| 🏗️ Gray Infrastructure | 🌿 Green Infrastructure |
|------------------------|------------------------|
| Drainage systems, underground tunnels | Wetlands, parks, gardens |
| Water treatment plants | Rain gardens, bioswales |
| Underground water tanks | Permeable pavements, green roofs |

---

## Slide 6 — Why Do We Need Sponge Cities? (Benefits)
**Four key benefits:**

| Benefit | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| 🌧️ **Prevent flooding** | Absorbs heavy rainwater, reduces pressure on drainage systems |
| 💧 **Store and reuse water** | Collected rainwater for irrigation, toilet flushing, cooling systems |
| ❄️ **Cool the city air** | Plants and water surfaces lower urban heat island effect |
| 🫧 **Clean dirty water** | Soil and plants filter pollutants, improving water quality |

---

## Slide 7 — Our Sponge City Design Plan for Guangzhou (Core!)
**Design elements we propose:**

| Design Element | Function | Key Feature |
|----------------|----------|-------------|
| 🛣️ **Permeable sidewalks / roads** | Rainwater soaks directly into the ground | Each m² can absorb **4,000 L/hour** |
| 🏠 **Green roofs** | Absorbs rooftop rainwater + insulation | Reduces surface runoff, lowers indoor temperature |
| 🌸 **Rain gardens** | Collects and filters rainwater | Uses plants and soil to clean pollutants |
| 🌾 **Bioswales (ecological ditches)** | Guides rainwater, filters debris | Sloped channels direct water underground |
| 🏞️ **Artificial wetlands / eco-parks** | Large-scale rainwater storage + public leisure space | e.g., Chongqing Tongziba Wetland Park (99 hectares) |
| 🏊 **Underground water storage tanks** | Stores large volumes of rainwater for later use | Supplies irrigation, toilet flushing, and cooling water |
| 🌊 **Ecological lakes / retention ponds** | Regulates water volume during storms | Acts as a scenic lake normally, a temporary reservoir during heavy rain |

---

## Slide 8 — Expected Outcomes
**The change Guangzhou will see:**

- ✅ Reduced urban flooding (target: absorb 70% of rainwater)
- ✅ Saved tap water (rainwater reuse for irrigation and cleaning)
- ✅ Lower summer city temperatures (cooling effect from green infrastructure)
- ✅ Improved water quality and urban environment
- ✅ More green recreational spaces, better quality of life for residents

---

## Slide 9 — Limitations and Challenges (Bonus Points!)
**Being honest about the problems:**

- ⚠️ Mainly effective for **light and moderate storms**; limited against extreme weather
- ⚠️ High construction cost and requires long-term maintenance
- ⚠️ Must work together with existing gray infrastructure (cannot rely only on green solutions)
- 📌 Reference: **Zhengzhou Flood (2021)** — even the best system cannot survive a once-in-a-millennium storm

---

## Slide 10 — Summary
**Summary:**
> A Sponge City is not a single project — it is a design philosophy: **coexist with water, rather than fight it.** Guangzhou has abundant rainwater resources. With proper design, we can turn a "water disaster" into a "water resource."
